# Step 5 — Atman = Brahman

*Tradition: Advaita Vedanta · Texts: Isha/Brihadaranyaka Upanishad peace verse ("Purnamadah"), Chandogya ("tat tvam asi"), Panchadashi (Vidyaranya) · Source talks: Purnam, Mandukya Part 2*

---

## Where we are

- **Step 1** split experience into awareness (subject) and objects — leaving **two** things.
- **Step 3.2** argued awareness alone has intrinsic existence.
- **Step 4** explained why we don't see this.

Step 5 finishes the job Step 1 started: it shows objects are **not a second reality**. Dualism collapses into non-duality.

---

## Summary

Objects never exist apart from awareness. So they are awareness itself, appearing in those forms. The awareness you are (**Atman**) and the reality behind the world (**Brahman**) are one and the same. **Tat tvam asi — you are that.**

---

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Atman | Reality as it appears as *you*, the awareness |
| Brahman | Reality as the ground of the *whole world* |
| Purnam | The full, the whole, the limitless |
| Mithya | Appearance — neither real nor unreal |
| Ishvara | Brahman/Turiya together with maya — God |

---

## 5.1 The leftover problem

After Step 1:

```
   AWARENESS   |   OBJECTS
   (subject)   |   (world, body, mind)
```

That's still two. That's **Samkhya** dualism: pure consciousness (*purusha*) and nature (*prakriti*). Advaita says this is a stage, not the end.

---

## 5.2 The key question

Swami quotes a teacher (in Hindi):

> "It's true that the seer is distinct from the seen. But have you ever considered — **is the seen distinct from the seer?**"

Normally distinctness runs both ways. Step 5 shows that here, it doesn't.

---

## 5.3 The test for distinctness

> **Two things are truly separate if each can be experienced without the other.**

### Example: microphone and cloth
- Can you experience the microphone without the cloth? Yes.
- Can you experience the cloth without the microphone? Yes.
- → Two separate things.

### Apply it to awareness and objects
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can awareness be present without objects? | **Yes** — deep sleep, samadhi (Step 2) |
| Can objects be experienced without awareness? | **Never.** "Experienced" already includes awareness. |

The dependence runs **one way only**. Objects never stand on their own apart from awareness.

> **Conclusion:** objects are not a second reality. They are awareness appearing to itself in those forms.

---

## 5.4 Examples of "one appearing as many"

### Gold and ornaments
- A necklace is gold through and through. There's no "necklace-stuff" besides gold.
- Melt it — gold remains. The necklace was a name and form of gold.

### Clay and pot
- "Clay produced a pot." But look at the pot: clay, clay, clay throughout.
- Nothing new came into existence. Clay took a shape.

### The monitor
- A screen shows cars, stars, people.
- Inside the monitor: no cars, no stars. Only the screen, taking those shapes.

### The mirror
- A mirror reflects the whole room: people, chairs, lights.
- What is actually there? Only glass.

### The dream (Step 3.3, now applied fully)
- Every person in your dream was your mind.
- Not "my mind plus some outside material" — **only** your mind.

### The child and the sky
- A child looks up and sees a blue dome — a surface.
- There is no surface; it's empty space.
- The eye, whose nature is to see, "sees" a surface where there's none.
- Likewise awareness, whose nature is to experience, experiences itself as objects.

## 5.5 The Purnam verse

> *Purnam adah, purnam idam, purnat purnam udachyate, purnasya purnam adaya, purnam evavashishyate.*
> That is full; this is full. From the full, the full arises. Taking the full from the full, the full alone remains.

### Line by line

| Phrase | Literal | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| *Purnam adah* | That (beyond the senses) is full | "That" = the transcendent, taken on faith (*paroksha*) |
| *Purnam idam* | This (perceived world) is full | "This" = what we see directly (*pratyaksha*) |
| — | Both called "full" | **Negates the gap** between remote and immediate — same reality |
| *Purnat purnam udachyate* | From the full, the full arises | "From" = cause; "full" = effect |
| — | Cause and effect both "full" | **Negates causality** — nothing truly created |
| *Purnasya purnam adaya…* | Recognizing the full in the full, only the full remains | Recognize Brahman in the world → only Brahman remains |

### The non-dualist's argument against dualism
- Dualist and non-dualist agree: God/Brahman is real.
- Dualist: God created a **separate, real** world.
- Non-dualist: anything *other than* the real must be **unreal**. Ironically, the dualist's separate world ends up as falsity.
- Non-dualist: the world isn't other than Brahman — it **is** Brahman appearing.

### A practical consequence: "never limit God"
If reality is truly non-dual, *everything* experienced is that one reality. So:
- God only formless → incomplete. God only with form → incomplete.
- Only male → incomplete. Only human form → incomplete.
- Hence Hinduism's range: fish, tortoise, man-lion, male, female, both (Ardhanarishvara), and formless.
- Sri Ramakrishna disliked dogmatism (*matuar buddhi*): never set limits on what the infinite can be.

## 5.6 Atman = Brahman

| Name | Same reality, seen as… |
|---|---|
| **Atman** | You — the awareness |
| **Brahman** | The ground of the whole universe |

- *Tat tvam asi* — you are that.
- *Aham brahmasmi* — I am Brahman.

### Ishvara and the individual (Mandukya Q&A)
| | Composition |
|---|---|
| Individual (*jiva*) | Turiya + an individual body-mind |
| God (*Ishvara*) | Turiya + total maya (creating the universe) |
| Remove the coverings | **Same Turiya** |

So "you are God" doesn't mean this person is God. It means the awareness at your core is the awareness at the core of God.

### Example: the Sufi poet
"When I searched for God, I found myself. When I searched for myself, I found God."

## 5.7 Three views of the world (*Panchadashi*)

| Viewer | The world is… | Sanskrit |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary person | Fully real, undeniable | *vastavi* |
| Inquirer / philosopher | Neither real nor unreal — appearance | *anirvachaniya, mithya* |
| Realized person | No separate world; Brahman alone | — |

### Example: what is a "lie"?
- Telling the truth, staying silent, telling a lie.
- A lie isn't nothing (something *was* said), but it isn't true.
- Falsity (*mithya*) is like that: not nothing, not ultimately real.

### Sri Ramakrishna: "Is the world false?"
"Why should it be false? That's a stage in inquiry." — Not denying falsity, not affirming reality: falsity is the middle (inquirer's) view, not the last word.

### The elderly Swami
Asked "Do the enlightened see Brahman, with the world like a shadow hanging around?" — *"Why should it be like that? There is only one."*

### Two equivalent liberating attitudes (Swami Girijananda)
Body, mind and world are all presented to awareness at once. So either:
- **"I am none of it"** — they are movies in me (Step 1's insight), or
- **"I am all of it"** — they are me appearing (Step 5's insight).

Both free you. **Bondage** is the third option: "I am only *this* one, and everything else is separate."

## 5.8 Practical consequences

### Ethics
- Everyone you meet is fundamentally you — the good, the bad and the ugly.
- "Whom will you criticize? Whom will you hate?"
- Saints show *sama-darshana*: equal vision toward friend and enemy, mud and gold (Sri Ramakrishna throwing both money and earth into the Ganga).

### The student's question (IITK Q&A)
"If I'm only the witness, why feel sympathy? Why not be indifferent — or even harm others?"
- First: you'd be equally indifferent to your *own* urges to harm.
- Second: from Step 5's side — "I am all of it" — others' suffering registers as your own. Saints respond to suffering instinctively.
- Some realized souls do seem detached; their mind, shaped by long practice, keeps helping out of momentum, like a fan that keeps turning after it's switched off.

### Suffering — Sri Ramakrishna's cancer
- Dying of throat cancer, he says he's in pain and can't eat.
- A young disciple says: "But I see you are in bliss."
- Ramakrishna laughs: "The rascal has found me out!"
- The pain was real. So was the peace beneath it — awareness that lights up the pain, untouched by it.

---

## 5.9 A connection: Purnam and Shunyam

- Buddhism (Madhyamaka, Nagarjuna): *shunyam*, emptiness; samsara and nirvana appear in the clear light of the void.
- Advaita: *purnam*, fullness; samsara and moksha appear in limitless awareness.
- Swami Saradananda: what Buddhists call shunyam, we call purnam.
- Sri Ramakrishna on the Buddha: he couldn't express in language what he found. "Between 'is' and 'is not' — that is exactly right."
- *(Both sides contest this equation — note it as a bridge, not a settled fact.)*

## Check against experience

Step 5.3 rests on one claim: **objects never exist unexperienced.**

A realist says: "The moon exists when nobody is looking." How do you think Advaita would reply?

## Contested points

1. **The realist objection** (above) — the biggest pressure on Step 5.
2. "Can't be experienced without awareness" may only prove we can't *know* unexperienced objects — not that they don't exist.
3. Whether Purnam and Shunyam truly coincide.

## Bridge to Step 6

Step 5 says the world is **appearance** — Brahman appearing as many. But that raises hard questions:
- What *is* this appearing power?
- Where does it reside? Is it real or unreal?
- Why does it look so solid, painful and convincing?

Advaita's name for it is **Maya** — the most criticized idea in the whole system.
