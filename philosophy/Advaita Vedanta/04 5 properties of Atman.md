# Step 3 — Five Properties of the Atman

*Tradition: Advaita Vedanta · Text: Bhagavad Gita, chapter 2 (verses ~11–25) · Source talk: The Atman*

---

## Where we are

- **Step 1:** awareness is distinct from every object.
- **Step 2:** awareness is constant across waking, dream and deep sleep.

Step 3 asks: **what must such awareness be like?** Each of the five properties follows from what came before — and one of them introduces a new argument that Step 5 will need.

---

## Summary

The Atman (the self as pure awareness) is:

| # | Property | Sanskrit | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Eternal | *nitya, avinashi* | Not born, does not die |
| 2 | The only reality | *atma satyam, jagat mithya* | Everything else borrows existence from it |
| 3 | All-pervading | *sarva-vyapi* | Everything appears within it |
| 4 | Not an object | *aprameya* | Cannot be seen or thought, yet undeniable |
| 5 | Non-doer, non-experiencer | *akarta, abhokta* | Body-mind acts; awareness only witnesses |

---

## Context: why the Gita starts here

Arjuna, on the battlefield of Kurukshetra, breaks down at the thought of killing his teachers and relatives. Krishna's **first** teaching is not about God, devotion or duty — it is about **what Arjuna really is**. Swami's point: everything else in the Gita comes after this.

---

## 3.1 Eternal (*nitya, avinashi*)

### The claim
Gita 2.12: There was never a time when you, I, or these kings were not — nor will there ever be a time when we cease to be.

This is the exact opposite of our intuition: "Before birth I wasn't; after death I won't be."

### The logic: two kinds of absence
| Absence | Sanskrit | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Before something exists | *pragabhava* | The pot before the potter makes it |
| After something is destroyed | *dhvamsabhava* | The pot after it is smashed |

The body has both. **The Atman has neither.**

### Example: childhood, youth, old age (Gita 2.13)
- The body is a child, then a teenager, then young, then old — enormous change.
- You say "*I* was in that child's body," "*I* am in this body now."
- If you don't treat each of those changes as *your* birth and death, why treat the final change that way? Death is one more transition.

### Example: changing clothes (Gita 2.22)
- We discard worn-out clothes and put on new ones.
- Likewise, a worn-out body is discarded and a new one taken.
- **Careful:** what moves on is the *subtle body* (mind, tendencies). The Atman doesn't move.

### Example: the old monk's cremation
At Belur Math, when a senior monk dies, monks sit through the night by the pyre singing — not grieving. One song: *O death, what fear do you hold for me? I was a child, I am young, soon I'll be old; with the body's end comes another body.* Death as a comma, not a full stop.

### Example: messengers of death
A dying man protests he got no warning. Death's messengers reply: the graying hair, the falling teeth, the dimming eyes — those were the warnings.

## 3.2 The only reality (*atma satyam, jagat mithya*)

**This is the most important property — Step 5 depends on it.**

### First: intrinsic vs. accidental properties

| Kind | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Intrinsic | Belongs to the thing; never gained or lost | Fire's heat |
| Accidental | Gained and lost | A pot's paint color — white today, red tomorrow |

### Example: the hot potato
- A potato boiling in water is hot. But the heat is **borrowed** from the water.
- The water borrows heat from the pan.
- The pan borrows heat from the fire.
- **The fire is hot by itself.**
- Potato, water, pan were cold before and will be cold again. The fire never "becomes" cold while it burns.

> **Rule:** If a property can be gained or lost, it's borrowed. If it can never be gained or lost, it's intrinsic.

### Apply the rule to *existence*
- Anything born and destroyed **gains and loses existence** → its existence is borrowed.
- The Atman is never born and never dies (3.1) → it never gains or loses existence → **existence is its very nature**.
- Advaita's word for this: ***sat*, pure being.**

*(Swami notes a philosopher would object that existence isn't a property. He sets that aside — keep it in mind.)*

### The consequence (Gita 2.16)
*The unreal never comes into being; the real never ceases to be.*

- If you are existence itself, then whatever is **not-you** (*anatma*) has no existence of its own.
- Yet we experience it. So it must **borrow** existence from you — like the potato from the fire.
- Hence: ***atma satyam, jagat mithya*** — the self is real; the world is appearance.

### Example: rope and snake
- In dim light a rope looks like a snake.
- The snake has no existence of its own — it is the rope, appearing as a snake.
- It's not *nothing* (you really saw something) — but it's not a real snake.

## 3.3 All-pervading (*sarva-vyapi*)

### The claim
Gita 2.17: Know that to be indestructible by which all this is pervaded.

Intuition says: "I'm here, not there. How can I be everywhere?"

### Example: the dream city
- Remember a vivid dream: streets, trees, a lake, crowds of people.
- On waking: *all* of it was made of your mind. The roads, the sky, the other people, the time and space of the dream — all you, the dreamer.
- So the dreamer "pervaded" the whole dream world.

### Apply it
Your life = awareness + its objects. What is true of the dreamer and the dream is true of awareness and everything it knows.

### Example: the monk in the Himalayas
A monk objects during a class: "I'm here, not there — how can I be all-pervading?"
The teacher: **"But aren't both 'here' and 'there' in your awareness?"**
- "I'm here, not there" is spoken as the **body**.
- In a dream, if someone said "you are everything here," you'd object the same way — until you woke up.

## 3.4 Not an object (*aprameya*)

### The claim
The Atman is not an object of any instrument of knowledge (*pramana*).

| Instrument | What it grasps | Grasps the Atman? |
|---|---|---|
| Eyes | Forms | No — Atman has no form |
| Ears | Sounds | No |
| Touch, taste, smell | Their objects | No |
| Mind | Concepts | No — it isn't a concept |

### The objection
"If no instrument can grasp it, what right do you have to say it exists? I could equally claim Harry Potter exists — but he's in the fiction section."

### The answer: self-luminous (*svaprakasha*)
It isn't an object — it is the **subject**, the one using every instrument.

### Example: the missing photographer
- An old group portrait. Everyone is in it — except the photographer.
- How do you know there was one? **The photograph itself.**
- Likewise: every experience you have is proof of awareness, though awareness never appears *in* the experience.

### Example: the doubter
Shankara: the Atman is the very self of the one who doubts it. To doubt awareness, you must be aware.

> **Practical point:** don't look for the Atman as a thing — not "out there," and not even "in here." "In here" and "out there" both appear *in* it.

## 3.5 Non-doer, non-experiencer (*akarta, abhokta*)

### The claim
Gita 2.19: Whoever thinks it slays, and whoever thinks it is slain — neither understands. It neither slays nor is slain.

- **Awareness + body-mind** = doer and experiencer.
- **Awareness by itself** = neither.

### Example: Emerson's "Brahma"
Ralph Waldo Emerson wrote a poem, "Brahma," closely based on this verse — the "red slayer" being the warrior of the Gita.

### Example: the film and the screen
- The film may be a tragedy or a comedy. The screen is untouched by the plot.
- More than untouched — **the screen makes the film possible**.
- You are the Atman, free of samsara — yet samsara happens *because of* you.

### Karma
- Vivekananda: whoever wears a form wears the chain of karma — but beyond name and form, the Atman is ever free.
- Gita 13.29: Nature (*prakriti*) does all actions; one who sees the self as non-doer truly sees.

### Not an excuse to stop acting
Krishna teaches "you are the non-doer" — and then tells Arjuna to **fight**. With body and mind, you cannot avoid acting. Act fully, for the welfare of the world, knowing yourself as witness.

| Path | Same idea, expressed as |
|---|---|
| Knowledge (*jnana*) | "Nature acts; I am the witness." |
| Devotion (*bhakti*) | "All my actions are offered to the Lord." |

## How the five fit together

| Property | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| 3.1 Eternal | Directly from Step 2 (constant across all states) |
| 3.2 Only reality | **New argument** (intrinsic existence) — a claim about the *world* |
| 3.3 All-pervading | Applies 3.2 via the dream |
| 3.4 Not an object | Directly from Step 1 |
| 3.5 Non-doer | Applies 3.4 to action and karma |

---

## Practical implications (from the talk's close)

- **Fear of death** diminishes — not disappears, but diminishes.
- **Hatred** loses its ground — if the Atman is one, whom will you hate?
- **The world's weight** lightens — it "shimmers," since it is not real apart from you.
- **Vivekananda:** each soul is potentially divine — *potentially*, because it must be realized and manifested in daily life. Not "I'm the Atman" and then losing your temper at every irritation.

## Bridge to Step 4

If you are eternal, real, all-pervading, untouched awareness — **why doesn't it feel that way?** Right now you feel like a small, mortal person in a large world.

Something is blocking the recognition. What is it, and how is it removed?
