# Step 1 — Seer vs. Seen (*Drg-Drishya Viveka*)

*Tradition: Advaita Vedanta · Text: Drg-Drishya Viveka · Source talk: Purnam*

---

## Where we are

This is the starting point. We assume nothing except ordinary experience. The aim is to sort experience into two sides and see which side "I" actually falls on.

---

## Summary

Every experience has two sides: **the one who experiences** (subject, *drashta*) and **what is experienced** (object, *drishya*). Advaita's first move is to sort carefully what belongs where. The result: body and mind move to the object side. Only awareness remains on the subject side.

---

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Drashta* / *drk* | The seer, the subject |
| *Drishya* | The seen, the object |
| *Viveka* | Discrimination — telling apart |

---

## 1.1 The structure of every experience

Look at anything right now — a cup on the table.

- There is **the cup** (what is seen).
- There is **the seeing of it** (you, the one aware of it).

This structure never changes, whatever the content: seeing a cup, hearing music, feeling hungry, remembering childhood. Always: *I am aware of X.*

---

## 1.2 The rule

> **Whatever you can experience is an object. Awareness is always on the subject side.**

Why? Because to experience something, there must be something *other than it* doing the experiencing. The eye sees everything except itself.

---

## 1.3 Applying the rule, layer by layer

Move inward one layer at a time and test each layer.

### Layer 1 — The world
- **Example:** The room, the traffic noise, the smell of coffee.
- **Test:** Can I experience it? Yes.
- **Result:** Object. (Nobody disputes this.)

### Layer 2 — The body
- **Example:** You feel your knee ache. You see your hands typing. You notice you are hungry.
- **Test:** Can I experience it? Yes — through every sense. I can see it, touch it, hear my heartbeat.
- **Swami's example:** To your doctor, your body is an object — scanned, tested, prodded. Look honestly: it is an object to *you* too. A remarkable biological machine — but a thing you are aware of.
- **Result:** Object.

### Layer 3 — The senses
- **Example:** Your eyesight blurs when you are tired. You notice you cannot hear well in a noisy room.
- **Test:** Can I notice the condition of my senses? Yes.
- **Result:** Object.

### Layer 4 — The mind
- **Example:** Anger arises in a meeting, then fades. A memory of school pops up. You notice you are distracted.
- **Test:** Can I notice thoughts and moods coming and going? Yes. You can say "I *had* an angry thought."
- **Result:** Object.

### Layer 5 — The "I-thought" (ego)
- **Example:** The thought "I am Madan, a researcher" — it appears when you introduce yourself, and is absent when you are absorbed in a hard problem.
- **Test:** Does it come and go? Yes.
- **Result:** Object.

### What remains
The awareness in which all five layers appear. It never shows up as one more item on the list.

```
World      → object
Body       → object
Senses     → object
Mind       → object
"I"-thought → object
─────────────────────
Awareness  → subject (the only thing left)
```

---

## 1.4 Supporting observations

### The dream
- In a dream, you experience a world, a body, people, events.
- Your physical body is lying in bed and appears **nowhere** in the dream.
- **Point:** Experiencing continues without *this* body being experienced. So "I" cannot simply be this body.

### Changes over a lifetime
- **Example:** You at 10, at 20, now. Body completely changed. Beliefs, likes, knowledge changed.
- Yet you say "*I* was that child."
- **Point:** Something stayed constant while body and mind changed. Changing things cannot be that constant.

### The cup of coffee
- **Example:** Groggy in the morning; alert after coffee.
- Does *awareness* get sharper? Advaita says no — the **mind** got sharper. Awareness revealed the groggy mind, then revealed the alert mind, unchanged both times.

---

## 1.5 The light metaphor (and its limits)

Awareness is often compared to light:
- **What fits:** light shows things without being changed by them — it falls on a flower and on garbage alike.
- **What doesn't fit:** physical light is itself an object; it is located; it can be absent; it doesn't know itself.
- **Advaita's term:** awareness is *svaprakasha* — **self-revealing**. A lamp shows a pot, but you need eyes to see the lamp. You need nothing further to know you are aware.

Swami's own caution: "We are light" does not mean we are photons. It is a pointer, nothing more.

---

## 1.6 Common objections

| Objection | Advaita's reply |
|---|---|
| "Consciousness is produced by the brain." | Not established — this is the hard problem of consciousness. The brain affects the *mind's contents*; that it produces *awareness* is unproven. |
| "Can't I observe my own awareness?" | What you observe is always some content — a thought *about* awareness. The observing itself is never the observed. |
| "Isn't this just introspection?" | No. Introspection is one thought looking at another thought. Awareness is what lights up both. |

---

## 1.7 The limit of this step

At this point we have **two** things: awareness here, objects there. That is still **dualism** — essentially the Samkhya position. Advaita doesn't stop here; Step 5 resolves it. For now, just be clear about the separation.

---

## Check against experience

Try to find **anything** in your experience right now that sits on the *subject* side — other than awareness itself.

If you find something, bring it back: either it is an object you haven't noticed, or it is a genuine objection to Step 1.

---

## Contested point

Whether "the knower can never be known" is strictly true. Some Western philosophers argue awareness *can* be aware of itself reflexively. Advaita agrees awareness is self-evident — but denies it is ever an *object*.

---

## Bridge to Step 2

Step 1 worked **within a single moment**: right now, awareness is distinct from what it knows.

But awareness might be just a passing feature of the waking state. What happens when the waking world disappears entirely — in dream, in deep sleep? Does awareness persist when *every* object is gone?
