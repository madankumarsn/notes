# 07 — Daily Practice: A Detailed Guide

*Drawn from Steps 1–6. Each practice says what to do, why it works (with the step it comes from), and an example. Start with one or two practices, not all eight.*

---

## The one idea behind everything

Advaita says the problem isn't your life. It's a **mistaken identity**: you take yourself to be a limited, separate person (body, mind, personality) when you are actually the **awareness** in which all of that appears.

Every practice below is a way of **testing that claim in your own experience**, and of loosening the mistaken identity a little at a time. You don't have to believe it first. Treat each one as an experiment.

Two terms you'll see:
- **Witness / awareness:** the "you" that is aware of thoughts, feelings, sensations and the world.
- **Object:** anything you can be aware of, including your body, emotions and thoughts.

---

## Practice 1 — Separate the one who knows from what is known
*Source: Step 1 (Seer vs. Seen)*

**The idea.** Every experience has two sides: the thing experienced (a cup, a sound, an anxious feeling) and you, who are aware of it. The rule from Step 1: *whatever you can experience is an object; awareness is always on the subject side.* The eye sees everything except itself.

**How to do it (2 minutes, any time).**
1. Notice something in the room, such as a cup or a sound.
2. Notice the **awareness of it**, the "seeing" or "hearing" itself.
3. Move inward, one layer at a time, and ask at each: *can I notice this?*
   - the room and world → yes, so it's an object
   - my body (an aching knee, hunger) → yes, so it's an object
   - my senses (blurry eyes when tired) → yes
   - my mind (anger arising, then fading; a distracted state) → yes
   - the thought "I am Madan, a researcher" → yes, since it comes and goes
4. Whatever is left is the one aware of all of it, and it never shows up as one more item on the list.

**Use it in daily life.** When a feeling grabs you, change the sentence:
- Instead of "**I am** anxious," try "**I am aware of** anxiety."
- You aren't pushing the feeling away. You're noticing there's room around it.

**Example from the notes.** Groggy in the morning, alert after coffee. Did *awareness* get sharper? Advaita says no. The mind did. Awareness showed the groggy mind and then the alert mind, unchanged both times.

**Test it.** Try to find anything in your experience, right now, that sits on the subject side other than awareness. If you find something, either it's an object you haven't noticed or it's a real objection to Step 1.

## Practice 2 — Notice the "rope and snake" misreadings
*Source: Step 4 (Ignorance and Error)*

**The idea.** At dusk you see a snake on the path. It's a rope.
- *Ignorance:* not seeing the rope clearly.
- *Error:* seeing a snake, with a racing heart.
- You can't beat the snake away with a stick. Only a light (knowledge) fixes it, and nothing is removed. The snake was never there.

In Advaita the big "snake" is the belief "I am only this limited person, separate from everything, born and going to die." Smaller snakes show up daily.

**How to do it.** When a strong reaction hits (fear, jealousy, a slight, dread before a meeting):
1. Pause and ask: **"What am I actually seeing, and what am I adding?"**
2. Separate the facts (an email with no reply, a colleague who didn't greet me) from the story (they're ignoring me, I'm failing).
3. Ask whether the story is a rope seen as a snake.

**Why this matters.** Many reactions are caused by misreading, not by the facts. The cure is clearer seeing, not forcing yourself to feel differently.

## Practice 3 — Recognize the screen while the movie plays
*Source: Steps 4.7 and 5*

**The idea.** A village boy at the cinema asks "where's the screen?" Wherever his father points, there's a picture: Krishna, the chariot, the sky. At the intermission, the lights come on and he sees the screen. After that, he knows it's there even when the film resumes.

The claim: you can recognize the screen **without stopping the film**. You don't need to quit your job, stop thinking, or reach a special state. Advaita says to use the mind and reason in ordinary waking life.

**How to do it (during any busy or stressful moment).** Use either attitude, as Swami Girijananda describes:
- **"I am none of it."** Body, mind and events are all appearing *in* awareness, like pictures on a screen. Or
- **"I am all of it."** All of it is awareness appearing as this form (as a pot is clay, an ornament is gold).

**The trap (bondage).** The third option: "I am only *this* one, and everything else is separate." The practice is to notice when you slip into that.

**Example.** Stuck in a stressful meeting. Silently: "this is a movie playing on the screen of my awareness." The meeting still matters and you still do your best. You stop being swallowed by it.

## Practice 4 — Act fully, but as the witness
*Source: Step 3.5 (non-doer)*

**The idea.** The Atman (awareness) is a witness, "the screen" that the film plays on, untouched by the plot. But the notes say clearly this is **not an excuse to stop acting**. Krishna tells Arjuna "you are the non-doer" and then tells him to **fight**. With a body and mind you can't avoid acting. So: act fully, for the welfare of the world, knowing yourself as witness.

**Two versions of the same practice.**
| Path | What you tell yourself |
|---|---|
| Knowledge (*jnana*) | "Nature acts; I am the witness." |
| Devotion (*bhakti*) | "All my actions are offered to the Lord." |

**How to do it.**
- Before a task: set your best effort as the goal, not a specific outcome.
- After it: notice the urge to be proud or crushed, and remind yourself the result is not the measure of you.
- Pick whichever wording feels natural. They point at the same thing.

## Practice 5 — Treat others as yourself
*Source: Step 5.8 (Ethics)*

**The idea.** If awareness is one and everyone is that awareness appearing in different forms, then "everyone you meet is fundamentally you, the good, the bad and the ugly." Advaita's question: **"Whom will you criticize? Whom will you hate?"** Saints show *sama-darshana*, equal vision toward friend and enemy, mud and gold.

**How to do it.**
- When you feel like criticizing or holding a grudge, pause and ask the question above.
- With a difficult person, remind yourself that the same awareness is looking out through them.
- Notice your own harmful urges the same way you'd notice theirs. The notes say you'd be equally indifferent to those.

**Common objection (from the notes).** "If I'm just the witness, why feel sympathy? Why not be indifferent?" Reply: from the "I am all of it" side, others' suffering registers as your own. Saints respond to suffering instinctively, like a fan that keeps turning after it's switched off.

## Practice 6 — In pain, separate the pain from the peace
*Source: Step 5.8 (Ramakrishna's illness)*

**The idea.** Sri Ramakrishna, dying of throat cancer, said he was in pain and couldn't eat. A young disciple said: "But I see you are in bliss." He laughed: "The rascal has found me out!" The pain was real. So was the peace beneath it, awareness that lights up the pain without being hurt by it.

**How to do it.**
1. When something hurts (physical or emotional), name it plainly: "this hurts."
2. Don't add the second layer: "and it's ruining everything / it will never end."
3. Notice the one who is aware of the pain is not itself in pain.

**Be honest about expectations.** This doesn't remove pain. It stops it from becoming your whole identity.

## Practice 7 — Spot Maya, the built-in contradictions of life
*Source: Step 6 (Maya)*

**The idea.** In Vivekananda's framing, Maya isn't a theory. It's a **statement of fact**: life is full of contradictions. Spotting them in the moment loosens their grip.

**What to watch for (from the notes).**
| Contradiction | In your day |
|---|---|
| Everything dies, yet we cling | Notice what you're trying to make permanent: career, reputation, a project |
| Pleasure promises satisfaction, gives a little, leaves you hungrier | Scrolling, snacks, shopping: notice the pull *before* and the feeling *after* |
| We feel free but are driven | Notice a habit that runs you |
| Attachment that hurts, yet we can't let go | A relationship or grudge you keep feeding |

**Examples.**
- **Narada's thirst:** he went for water, fell in love, married, raised a family, lost everything to a flood, and woke up to Krishna asking "I've been waiting half an hour." Daily life can feel that real and be that dreamlike.
- **The monk at Dakshineswar:** came out of his room laughing and dancing when monsoon clouds blew away: "Clear sky, dark clouds, clear sky again."

**How to do it.** When you catch one, don't judge it. Just say: *"And this is Maya."* Smile at it if you can.

## Practice 8 — Don't fear the "fear of death" or force it away
*Source: Step 3 (Eternal) and the talk's close*

**The idea.** Awareness is present across waking, dreaming and deep sleep (Step 2), and what is always present can't be "born" or "die." From this, **fear of death diminishes**, "not disappears, but diminishes," and hatred loses its ground. The world also "shimmers" and feels lighter, since it isn't real apart from you.

**How to do it.**
- Notice when worry about loss, aging or endings arises. Treat it as a thought to look at (Practice 1), not a verdict.
- Remember you've already seen your own states change: childhood, youth, old age. The one who noticed those changes stayed the same.

## The method that powers all of this: hearing → reflection → contemplation
*Source: Step 4.6*

| Stage | What you do | You know it's done when you say… |
|---|---|---|
| **Hearing** (*shravana*) | Study the teaching systematically | "I understand what it's saying." |
| **Reflection** (*manana*) | Raise every doubt and resolve it | "I'm convinced it's true." |
| **Contemplation** (*nididhyasana*) | Dwell on the truth you've understood | "I actually see it." |

Key points:
- **Don't skip order.** You can't doubt what you haven't understood, and you can't contemplate what you haven't been convinced of.
- **Contemplation before conviction is self-hypnosis.** Repeating "I am awareness" without understanding doesn't work.
- Vivekananda: repeat the truth to yourself "until it tingles in every drop of your blood", but only once you understand it.

## A realistic routine

**Week 1–2: Learn it.**
- Do **Practice 1** once a day (2 minutes).
- Read one step per evening and write one doubt.

**Week 3–4: Add reactions.**
- Add **Practice 2** (rope and snake) whenever you get a strong emotional reaction.
- Answer your written doubts (this is *reflection*).

**Week 5+: Add the rest.**
- Add Practices 3–7 as the situations arise. You don't schedule them.
- Weekly: re-read one step and its "Contested points."

**Daily evening check (5 minutes).**
1. What did I understand more clearly today?
2. Where did I misread a "rope" as a "snake"?
3. Where did I lose my temper or cling? (Vivekananda's warning: saying "I'm the Atman" and then losing your temper at every irritation is **not** the point. Realize it in daily life.)

---

## Cautions (from your own notes)
- Being the witness is **not** indifference or inaction. Act fully.
- Fear of death diminishes. It doesn't vanish.
- Some arguments in Advaita show *possibility*, not proof. Test them in your own experience, and re-read the "Contested points" in each step.
